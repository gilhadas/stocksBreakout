## Version History — What Changed in Each Version

Each version is **additive**: every new version keeps all previous improvements and adds one more dimension.

```
V1 → V2 → V3 → V4 → V5 → V6 → V7 → V8
             ↘ V5X → V6X → V8X
```

---

### V1 — Legacy Binary Momentum (Baseline)

**What it adds:** 4 binary technical checks: RSI > 50, MACD bullish crossover, ADX > 25 (trending), VWAP above price.

**How it scores:** Each check is a hard yes/no gate. If 3 of 4 pass → HIGH. All 4 → PREMIUM. Very blunt instrument.

**Backtest role:** The "baseline" in comparisons. Surprisingly strong (+141% in 2024–25) because simple momentum works in trending markets. Shows the floor — any modern version should beat it *risk-adjusted* even if not always raw return.

**Limitation:** No nuance. A RSI of 51 and RSI of 85 are treated identically. Doesn't capture pattern quality, overextension risk, or institutional-grade setups.

---

### V2 — Composite Momentum + Conviction Scoring

**What it adds over V1:**
- Replaces the 4 binary momentum checks with a **single composite momentum score** (RSI + MACD + ADX + ROC, blended into 0–100)
- Adds **conviction score** — measures *how strong* the breakout candle is (close position in range, volume surge ratio, gap presence, consecutive green candles)
- Composite momentum ≥ 50 → pass (13 pts); conviction ≥ 40 → pass (8 pts)

**Why this is better than V1:** A stock with RSI = 72, MACD accelerating strongly, ADX = 45, ROC surging scores much higher than one barely crossing each threshold. V2 captures *degree* of momentum, not just presence.

**Backtest role:** The **foundation for all modern versions** (V3–V8). When you see "V8 uses V2 base signals" it means: signals generated by the full modern scoring system, before any additional post-scan filter is applied.

---

### V3 — Bollinger Band Filter + 23 Pattern Detectors + SPY Hedge

**What it adds over V2:**
- **Bollinger Band trend filter**: requires price to be above the BB midline (SMA-20) for cleaner entries
- **23 pattern detectors**: 12 chart patterns (bull flag, ascending triangle, cup & handle, H&S, double bottom, etc.) + 11 candlestick patterns (hammer, engulfing, morning star, doji, etc.). Bullish pattern confirmed → 10 pts
- **Grade D rejection gate**: very low quality signals immediately rejected
- **SPY hedge option**: allocates a portion of capital to SPY for portfolio hedging

**Backtest role:** Primarily tests the pattern contribution and SPY hedge value. V3 signals = V2 signals (same pool), different sim configuration. In practice V3 shows the hedge costs returns in bull markets.

---

### V4 — Overextension Filter

**What it adds over V2:**
The single most impactful *risk-reduction* change: **reject breakouts where the stock has already moved too far above its SMA**.

#### What is "overextension"?

A stock is overextended when its price has run **too far above its trend line (SMA)** in too short a time. Think of it like a rubber band stretched to its limit — the further it's stretched, the more likely it snaps back.

**Example:** NVDA closes at $950. Its 150-day SMA is at $620. That's 53% above the SMA. Even if it "breaks out" above a recent high, buying here means you're buying at the top of a blow-off. The reward/risk is terrible.

**V4 thresholds (from config):**

| Mode | Mild | Heavy | REJECT |
|------|------|-------|--------|
| swing | > 10% above SMA | > 20% | > 25% |
| longterm | > 15% above SMA | > 25% | > 35% |

- **Mild** (10–20%): stock scores lower on `not_overextended` (partial penalty)
- **Heavy** (20–25%): larger penalty
- **REJECT** (> 25%): signal thrown out entirely, regardless of other scores

**Why does this matter so much for drawdown?** Chasing extended breakouts is the #1 cause of large losses. You buy at a blow-off top just before a 20–30% retracement. The overextension filter is essentially a "don't buy stretched rubber bands" rule. In the V6X backtest it reduced max drawdown from -22% to -14% while keeping returns strong.

**Backtest role:** V4 signals = V5X signals (same scan variant). Tested standalone to isolate the overextension filter's effect.

---

### V5 — 52-Week High Proximity + RSI Divergence + Sector Momentum

**What it adds over V2:**
- **Near 52-week high** (8 pts): stocks within 5% of their 52-week high tend to break to new highs. Minervini's research confirms this is where big moves originate.
- **RSI divergence** (5 pts): bullish divergence (price makes lower low, RSI makes higher low) — a hidden strength signal before the breakout
- **Sector momentum** (6 pts): if the sector ETF (XLK, XLE, XLF, etc.) is itself in an uptrend, individual names in that sector get a score boost

**Backtest role:** V5 signals = V2 signals (same pool, same scan). These scoring additions already live in the modern scanner — any signal you see today already receives these bonuses if applicable.

---

### V5X — V5 + Overextension Filter (First Combination)

**What it is:** Combines V5's scoring additions WITH V4's overextension filter.

**This is the overextension-filtered scan variant.** During the backtest's single-pass scan, `v5x_signals` = all signals found when `use_v4_overextension=True`. The overextension check happens **inside the detector**, potentially rejecting a signal entirely before it's even scored.

**Backtest role:** The `v5x_signals` pool is the shared base for V4, V5X, V6X, and V8X configs.

---

### V6 — 23 Patterns With Volume Confirmation Scoring

**What it adds over V5:**
- **Pattern volume confirmation** (6 pts): V3 already detected patterns and gave 10 pts for bullish patterns. V6 adds a *second* bonus: if the pattern's volume profile is correctly shaped (e.g., bull flag has contracting volume during the flag, then a spike on breakout), it earns 6 additional pts.
- All 23 patterns have a `volume_confirmed` boolean based on pattern-specific volume behavior (not just "volume > average")

**Why this matters:** A bull flag with perfect volume tapering and a high-volume breakout candle is a much higher-quality signal than a flag where volume was erratic. The 6-pt confirmation bonus helps surface these premium setups.

**Backtest role:** V6 signals = V5 signals (same pool — the extra scoring is already in the detector). V6-A configs use V5 signals pool with the same HIGH+/PREMIUM+ filtering.

---

### V6X — V6 + Overextension Filter (Best Pre-V7 Config)

**What it is:** V6 scoring + V4 overextension filter. Uses `v5x_signals` pool.

**Why this was the previous recommended production config:**
- Sharpe 2.62 (best of all versions tested)
- MaxDD only -13.56% (28% less than SPY's -18.76%)
- 60.3% win rate
- +41.45% above SPY

The overextension filter cost some raw return vs V1-A (+129% vs +141%) but dramatically improved risk-adjusted performance and nearly halved the max drawdown.

### V7 — Momentum Surge (Explosive Gap + Volume Catalyst)

**What it adds:**
A new signal TYPE alongside traditional breakouts. Momentum surge fires when:

```
(gap_up ≥ 5%  OR  intraday_move ≥ 5%  OR  daily_move_pct ≥ 5%)
AND  Vol_Ratio ≥ 3.0  (3× average volume)
```

**What "momentum-only" means in the backtest:**

V7 in the backtest tests ONLY the subset of signals where `is_momentum = True` — stocks that had an explosive move WITH massive volume, like:
- Earnings gaps: stock beats earnings and gaps up 10% at open
- News catalysts: FDA approval, major contract win, index inclusion
- Short squeezes: volume 5× avg + stock up 8% intraday

These are fundamentally different from consolidation breakouts. They don't need:
- Prior consolidation (bypassed)
- Liquidity gate (bypassed — the volume surge IS the liquidity proof)

**Why separate V7 from V2?** To measure: *does buying only explosive catalyst moves (not consolidation breakouts) produce better returns?* The backtest will tell us. In theory, momentum surges have high win rates early in the move but also mean-revert faster — the simulation will show which sizing/quality filter works best.

**Signal marker:** Momentum signals get `Type='Momentum'` in the output (vs `'Breakout'`).

### V8 — Minervini Stage 2 Trend Template (Proportional)

**What it adds:**
Mark Minervini's Stage 2 Trend Template — 8 conditions that define a stock in a healthy institutional uptrend.

#### The 8 Minervini Conditions:

| # | Condition | What it means |
|---|-----------|--------------|
| C1 | Price > SMA150 AND Price > SMA200 | Stock is above both medium and long-term averages |
| C2 | SMA150 > SMA200 | Medium-term average is above long-term (trend alignment) |
| C3 | SMA200 slope positive (20-day lookback) | Long-term trend is still *rising*, not just flat |
| C4 | SMA50 > SMA150 AND SMA200 | Short-term average above both — strong near-term momentum |
| C5 | Price > SMA50 | Stock is above its own short-term average |
| C6 | Price ≥ 25% above 52-week low | Institutional accumulation phase (not a fresh breakdown) |
| C7 | Price within 25% of 52-week high | Stock is near its highs, not deeply extended downward |
| C8 | RS positive (annual return > 0) | Stock has outperformed over the past year |

**How it scores — proportional (new in this update):**

Previously: binary — score ≥ 7 → 15 pts, else 0.
Now: `minervini_score / 8 × 15` — every condition contributes 1.875 pts:

| Conditions met | Score contribution |
|---|---|
| 8 / 8 | **15.0 pts** (full weight) |
| 7 / 8 | **13.1 pts** |
| 6 / 8 | **11.25 pts** |
| 5 / 8 | **9.4 pts** |
| 4 / 8 | **7.5 pts** |
| 0 / 8 | **0 pts** |

This means stocks in a strong uptrend (6+ conditions) get meaningful credit even without perfect Stage 2 alignment. A stock with 5 conditions might still make PREMIUM if all its other scores are strong.

**MinerviniScore (0–8) is always shown in signal output** — you can quickly see how "Stage 2 ready" any signal is.

**Why V2 base for the V8 backtest pool (not V1 or V5X)?**

The V8 backtest pool is: *V2 signals filtered to `MinerviniScore ≥ 7`*.

- **Not V1** — V1 is the legacy system, kept for historical comparison. All modern features build on V2's composite scoring.
- **Not V5X** — V5X already applies the overextension filter during scanning. Using V5X as the V8 base would combine TWO filters at once (overextension AND Minervini), making it impossible to isolate what Minervini alone contributes. That combination is tested separately as **V8X**.
- **V2 base** — the "clean slate" of the modern scoring system. V8 = V2 + Minervini filter only. This lets us answer: *"Does requiring Minervini Stage 2 alignment improve returns on top of standard composite scoring?"*

**V8X** separately tests V5X (overextension-filtered) signals with MinerviniScore ≥ 7 — the most selective combination of all filters.

---

### V8X — Minervini + Overextension Filter (Most Selective)

Uses the `v5x_signals` pool (overextension filter applied during scan) then additionally filtered to `MinerviniScore ≥ threshold`.

This is the most conservative filter: signals must pass:
1. Price breakout (mandatory gate)
2. Overextension check (V4): not > 25% above SMA
3. Minervini Stage 2 (V8): ≥ 7 of 8 conditions

Fewer signals, but potentially the highest quality. Expect lower signal count, higher win rate if the hypothesis holds.

## Debugging

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| No signals | Check `scanner_output/rejections/` for rejection reasons |
| IB connection fails | Use `--live` (yfinance) or ensure IB Gateway on port 7497 |
| Email not sending | Check `.env` has valid `GMAIL_APP_PASSWORD` |
| Backtest slow (first run) | Normal — fetches ~3 yrs of data. Subsequent runs use parquet cache (~50× faster) |
| Clear cache | `python -c "from yfinance_adapter import YFinanceAdapter; YFinanceAdapter().clear_disk_cache()"` |
| Stale signals | Check `score_adjustments.json` — learning loop may be downweighting features |
| Cron not logging | Verify `run_scanner.sh` has execute permission: `chmod +x run_scanner.sh` |
| macOS TCC / FDA error | Add `/Library/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/3.14/Resources/Python.app` to System Settings → Full Disk Access |
